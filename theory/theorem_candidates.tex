\section{Candidate propositions and Stage 2 results}

\subsection{Stage 2 Proposition: deterministic niche persistence}
\mstatus{PROVED} \istatus{VERIFIED for S6-AE} \nstatus{NOT CLAIMED}

\begin{proposition}[Occupied-niche persistence under the verified selector]
\label{prop:niche-persistence}
Fix a niche map $g$. At cycle $k$, suppose (i) every member of $P_k$ is in
$C_k$; (ii) $C_k$ is finite and unique by instance hash, with equal-hash
candidates niche-compatible under the declared union semantics; (iii)
$|\mathsf S(C_k)|\leq m\leq|C_k|$; (iv) $T_k=T_m(C_k)$ contains the complete
per-niche floor $Q(C_k)$; and (v) $P_{k+1}=T_k$. Then
\[
 \mathsf S(P_{k+1})=\mathsf S(C_k)\supseteq\mathsf S(P_k).
\]
If $C_k=\operatorname{unique}(P_k\cup O_k)$ under the same fixed map, then
more precisely
\[
 \mathsf S(P_{k+1})=
 \mathsf S(P_k)\cup\mathsf S(O_k).
\]
The statement remains true when some or all scheduled mutation attempts are
rejected, because rejection contributes no child and causes no resampling.
\end{proposition}

The assumptions match the inspected S6-AE selection path: the corrected C00
union and later parent--child unions contain all old parents; QD4,
normalization, centroids, and tie rules are fixed; at most 1,000 niches are
occupied while $m=10{,}000$; selection contains exactly one current-HGB elite
from every occupied candidate niche; and $P=T$. The completed run's strict
checks verify these operational invariants. This proves a niche-set invariant,
not parent-hash survival, within-niche structural equivalence, or future
reachability.

\begin{lemma}[Exact membership test for a candidate]
\label{lem:membership}
Under the deterministic selector in the formal model, a candidate survives
if and only if it is its niche's score elite (including the declared hash tie
rule), or it lies within the first $m-|Q(C)|$ positions of the global order on
$C\setminus Q(C)$. Hence neither membership in $P_k$ nor occupancy of its
niche is sufficient for that candidate to survive.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Stage 2 theorem candidate: a certified bridge chain}
\mstatus{PROVED under explicit premises} \istatus{PARTIAL/OPEN}
\nstatus{NOT CLAIMED}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-budget causal bridge-chain bound]
\label{prop:bridge-chain}
Fix a starting cycle $k$ and an integer $L\geq1$. Let the bridge sets
$B_0,\ldots,B_L$ be selected by the predictable, non-anticipating rule in
the formal model. Suppose $E_0$ holds almost surely. Let
$H_0=E_0$ and $H_{\ell+1}=H_\ell\cap G_\ell\cap R_\ell$.
For every $\ell=0,\ldots,L-1$ and every positive-probability
pre-generation history in $H_\ell$, assume
\[
 \Prb(G_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre})\geq a_\ell.
\]
On the same histories for which the conditional generation probability is
positive, assume the \emph{pre-generation-conditioned} retention bound
\[
 \Prb(R_\ell\mid
       \mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre},G_\ell)\geq\rho_\ell.
\]
Here the second conditional probability averages over the still-unresolved
mutation batch and any other explicitly modeled random inputs; it does not
condition on the fully realized scored candidate pool.
Alternatively, one may assume the stronger deterministic certificate:
for every admissible generated union containing a bridge member, the
selector retains a member of the designated bridge set, giving
$\rho_\ell=1$.

Assume the final, causally chosen bridge set satisfies
$B_L\subseteq\HARD_{k+L-1}$ almost surely for the entering checkpoint
$q_{k+L-1}$, without looking ahead to future solver states or the final
random mutation batch. Define the exact certified-chain success events
\[
 D^{\rm chain,gen}=H_{L-1}\cap G_{L-1},\qquad
 D^{\rm chain,acq}=H_{L-1}\cap G_{L-1}\cap R_{L-1}.
\]
These are subsets of the ordinary final-cycle presence events
$D_{k+L-1}^{\rm gen}$ and $D_{k+L-1}^{\rm acq}$ respectively. Then
\begin{align*}
 \Prb(D^{\rm chain,gen})
 &\geq \prod_{\ell=0}^{L-1}a_\ell
           \prod_{\ell=0}^{L-2}\rho_\ell,\\
 \Prb(D^{\rm chain,acq})
 &\geq \prod_{\ell=0}^{L-1}a_\ell\rho_\ell.
\end{align*}
No independence is required. If the declared deterministic charged costs
$c_k,\ldots,c_{k+L-1}$ sum to at most budget $B$, these are finite-budget
\emph{presence} bounds, not new-instance discovery bounds.

One sufficient operator-specific source for $a_\ell$ is a
pre-generation-measurable choice of a retained parent $x\in B_\ell$ and a
designated operator $o_\ell\in\mathcal O$ such that
\[
 \widetilde K_{k+\ell,o_\ell}
 (x,B_{\ell+1}\mid S_{k+\ell})\geq a_\ell
\]
on every admissible history, where the conditional kernel is defined under
the declared non-frozen random ensemble and includes rejection at
$\partial$. The all-operator deterministic batch attempts the designated
operator without any random parent/operator-selection factor.
To infer $G_\ell$ from the accepted operator output, equal hashes must
identify the same phenotype on that pool or at least preserve membership
in $B_{\ell+1}$; niche-label compatibility alone does not suffice for
the bridge theorem.
\end{proposition}

The deterministic retention certificate is directly testable using the
actual HGB/QD rank rule. Specifically, $\rho_\ell=1$ follows if every
admissible realized scored candidate union containing $B_{\ell+1}$ retains
a bridge member either as the tie-broken per-niche score elite or through
global fill (Lemma~\ref{lem:membership}). After the complete scored union is
known, the retention probability is the indicator
$\mathbf 1_{R_\ell}$, not an unexplained fractional probability.
A fractional $0<\rho_\ell<1$ requires a clearly declared ensemble and
a lower bound on retention conditioned only on pre-batch information and
the event $G_\ell$. Within-niche transition equivalence of possible elites
would be one \emph{additional} way to certify future reachability, not a
property guaranteed by QD occupancy or hardness prediction accuracy.

For S6-AE, the all-operator schedule, rejection handling, capacity inequality,
selector form, and $P=T$ map are verified. Positive, history-uniform
$a_\ell$ values, scientifically meaningful \emph{causally chosen} bridge
sets, and deterministic or probabilistic $\rho_\ell$ bounds are OPEN.
Changing BQ checkpoints are allowed, but terminal set inclusion must be
verified against the checkpoint already available before the final batch.
Freezing every bridge set at cycle $k$ would require the stronger uniform
terminal-inclusion guarantee over the possible future solver trajectories.

\paragraph{Repeated certified windows.}

If after every no-hit $L$-cycle block the next block again starts with $E_0$

\subsection{Mechanism-level comparator boundary}
\mstatus{CONDITIONAL FRAMEWORK} \istatus{OPEN} \nstatus{OPEN}
The bridge theorem permits a fair structural comparison only after each
method receives its own transition and cost model.

\begin{itemize}
\item A reset evolution strategy can erase intermediate bridge sets between
rounds, so a long path certificate may fail, but a reset distribution can
still have larger direct mass on the true-hard region.
\item A stationary generator has no inherited structural chain; its discovery
law is determined by its sampling mass on the cycle-specific hard region and
its per-sample cost. It can beat BLADE when that mass is sufficiently large.
\item A HAC-style method with random restarts requires a kernel on its full
gradient state, a restart law, stopping rules, and charged forward/backward
costs. One gradient step is not equated with one FOGA attempt or one full
110,000-attempt generation. The Stage 1 path counterexample shows only that a
useful gradient can win in a toy model; it is not a HAC superiority theorem.
\end{itemize}

Proposition~\ref{prop:niche-persistence} is a structural-coverage statement.
Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge-chain} concerns true-hardness generation or
acquisition only when its final bridge set lies in the declared true BQ hard
region and, for acquisition, survives selection. Neither QD occupancy nor HGB
score alone supplies that implication.

\subsection{Candidate 1: finite-window reachability comparison}
\mstatus{CONDITIONAL} \istatus{PARTIAL} \nstatus{NOT CLAIMED} The following
is the strongest surviving candidate.

\begin{proposition}[Memory advantage on a stable target window]
\label{prop:window}
Suppose the true hard region attached to the declared discovery event is
constant, $\HARD_j=\HARD$, for a window of $W$ comparison opportunities and
each opportunity has the same deterministic compute cost
$c>0$. On every no-hit history in that window, suppose a memory policy $M$
has conditional hit hazard at least $p_M$, and a comparator $G$ has
conditional hit hazard at most $p_G$, with $p_M>p_G$. Then, for
$1\leq n\leq W$,
\[
 \Prb(\tau_M\leq n)\geq 1-(1-p_M)^n
 > 1-(1-p_G)^n\geq \Prb(\tau_G\leq n),
\]
and, for $2\leq n\leq W$,
\[
 \Ex[c\min(\tau_M,n)]
 \leq c\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-p_M)^j
 < c\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-p_G)^j
 \leq \Ex[c\min(\tau_G,n)].
\]
For a generic policy that actually samples a parent, one sufficient
derivation of $p_M$ is the following: on each no-hit history,
with conditional probability at least $r$ a useful retained parent is
available; given that event it is selected with probability at least
$\gamma$; conditional on that selection, its mutation/evaluation reaches the
true current hard region with probability at least $\alpha$. Then
$p_M\geq r\gamma\alpha$. No independence among these events is assumed;
the bounds are conditional in the stated order.

That random-selection factorization is not the S6-AE schedule. For the
verified deterministic Cartesian batch, every retained parent receives all
11 operators. If $r$ lower-bounds availability of a useful parent and
$\alpha_{\rm batch}$ lower-bounds, under the explicitly declared RNG
ensemble, the conditional probability that its scheduled operator batch
generates the declared hit, then the appropriate sufficient bound is
$p_M\geq r\alpha_{\rm batch}$. With roots and all seeds conditioned to their
frozen values, this is a statement about a realized batch, not an estimated
random parent/operator-selection probability.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Importance.} It turns a structural-memory claim into a finite-budget,
equal-cost comparison that explicitly accounts for parent access and mutation
reachability. It also makes clear that survival alone is not a sufficient
mechanism.

\paragraph{Assumptions and realism.} A piecewise-stable target window and
equal-cost opportunities are useful first approximations. In S6-AE the BQ
checkpoint is fixed during mutation/acquisition in cycle $k$ and changes only
after $T_k$ is frozen and labeled, but comparison windows spanning adaptations
can still move $\HARD_k$. HGB40, archive, exact-label, and BQ-training costs
also differ from a single mutation attempt. The deterministic 10,000-by-11
schedule and $P=T$ update are verified; the history-uniform
$r\alpha_{\rm batch}$ bound, scientific threshold, and matched comparator
cost/hazard remain \istatus{OPEN}.

\paragraph{Proof approach.} Induction on the no-hit survival probability,
then the identity expressing expected truncated hitting time as the sum of
survival probabilities. It requires no conditional independence and no
Markov approximation. Proof details are in \texttt{proof\_attempts.tex}.

\paragraph{Counterexample and HAC distinction.} If memory's conditional
hazard is lower because its retained parents are stale, or if a gradient
proposal has larger hazard per compute unit, the premise fails. The exact
examples in \texttt{counterexamples.tex} show both. Thus this is a conditional
criterion that can distinguish BLADE from fixed generation or HAC only after
their hazards and costs are measured; it does not establish that BLADE wins.

\subsection{Candidate 2: inherited memory never hurts}
\mstatus{DISPROVED} \istatus{NOT APPLICABLE} \nstatus{NOT CLAIMED} The
proposed blanket claim is that retaining historical
parents cannot increase first-hitting cost relative to a fixed generator.
This is false even with two structural states and one target change. The
stale-memory example in Section~\ref{sec:counterexamples} has expected cost
$100$ for inherited memory versus $2$ for a fixed uniform generator. The
failure is not a technical gap in Candidate 1: the required hazard dominance
is reversed.

\paragraph{Importance and proof approach.} A universal no-harm theorem would
have supported the research objective directly, but the exact counterexample
rules it out. No proof is possible without additional target-drift,
forgetting, or reachability assumptions. These extra assumptions would need
to be stated and independently justified; they cannot be inferred from the
word ``memory.''

\paragraph{Assumptions and realism.} The universal claim would require memory never to reduce true-hardness reachability per unit cost for any target shift or parent-selection history. That is not guaranteed by inherited populations and is contradicted by the stale-memory example; realism for BLADE-NCO is low absent explicit target-aware forgetting and verified hazard bounds.

\paragraph{Strongest proof approach.} An exact finite-state counterexample proves the universal claim false. A positive restricted result would need additional drift and reachability premises, which are instead stated explicitly in Candidate 1.

\paragraph{HAC distinction.} Nothing in this false claim compares BLADE to
HAC. HAC can outperform a poorly aligned retained population, as the path
example in Section~\ref{sec:counterexamples} demonstrates.

\subsection{Candidate 3: diversity always improves discovery}
\mstatus{DISPROVED} \istatus{NOT APPLICABLE} \nstatus{NOT CLAIMED} The
proposed blanket claim is that preserving a more
diverse population always reduces first-hitting time. In a three-state model,
a diverse parent selector spends half of each equal-cost proposal on an
irrelevant niche and has expected hitting cost $2$, while a focused selector
hits in cost $1$. The exact construction is in Section~\ref{sec:counterexamples}.

\paragraph{Importance and assumptions.} Diversity can preserve useful future
routes, but its benefit depends on niche relevance, selection allocation,
target drift, and cost. A monotonicity proof from diversity cardinality alone
cannot work. This counterclaim concerns a universal guarantee, not a claim
that Elite-QD1000 behaves like the toy selector.

\paragraph{Realism and strongest proof approach.} No selection-allocation or niche-relevance condition is supplied for Elite-QD1000, so the premise is not verified for BLADE-NCO. The exact three-state construction is a complete disproof of the universal claim; a positive diversity theorem would require explicit target-access conditions. This does not rank BLADE against HAC.

\paragraph{HAC distinction.} It provides no general ranking against HAC.
With equal compute, a focused or gradient-guided policy may spend more
proposals on the current reachable hard region; the actual ranking depends
on transition probabilities and costs, neither of which is specified here.
