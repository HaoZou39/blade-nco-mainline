\section{Formal model and implementation boundary}

\subsection{Structural states and mutation}
Let $\mathcal{Z}$ be the set of encoded candidate instances and let
$\phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{X}$ be a measurable map from encodings to the
solver-visible TSP instance. For the inspected fixed-size experiments one may
take $\mathcal{X}$ to be the valid canonical subset of
$[0,1]^{100\times2}$, equipped with its Borel $\sigma$-algebra
$\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal X}$; a variable-size model may instead use a measurable disjoint
union over instance sizes. Encodings $z,z'$ are equivalent when
$\phi(z)=\phi(z')$. A structural state is a phenotype $x\in\mathcal{X}$,
for example a canonical graph/instance together with whichever structural
attributes determine solver input. The exact canonicalization and attributes
are implementation questions. We assume $\phi$ and every event used below are
measurable.

The instance space is not made finite by a finite search budget. A realized
finite-budget run contains finitely many attempted instances, but the support
of a continuous-coordinate mutation kernel can remain uncountable. For each
BQ cycle $k$ and FOGA operator $o$, adjoin an isolated rejection symbol
$\partial$ and write
$\overline{\mathcal X}=\mathcal X\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{\partial\}$ with the
natural extended sigma-algebra. Let
\[
 \widetilde K_{k,o}(x,\cdot\mid s)
\]
be a Markov kernel from a parent $x$ and full pre-mutation state $s$ into
$\overline{\mathcal X}$. An outcome in $\mathcal X$ is the valid repaired
phenotype of an accepted proposal; $\partial$ records a rejected or invalid
proposal. The kernel includes stochastic operator parameters, repair, and
canonicalization. Dependence on $k$ or $s$ permits solver-, archive-, or
history-dependent behavior. The corresponding encoding process need not
remain Markov after projection through $\phi$.

Let $d:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^4$ be the measurable QD descriptor map,
including the frozen normalization, and let
$g:\mathcal{X}\to\{1,\ldots,K\}$ be nearest-centroid assignment with a
declared measurable tie rule. Then
$\mathcal{N}_j=g^{-1}(\{j\})$ gives a finite measurable partition after
discarding any empty cells. Finiteness belongs to the niche index set, not to
$\mathcal{X}$.

\subsection{Two time scales and the S6-AE batch}
Use $k$ only for the BQ adaptation cycle. At the start of cycle $k$, the BQ
solver state $q_k$ and the parent population
$P_k=(X_{k,1},\ldots,X_{k,m})$ are fixed, with $m=10{,}000$ in S6-AE. Let
$P_k$ also denote its membership set when used inside $\subseteq$, $\cap$, or
$\mathsf S(\cdot)$; the ordered tuple is used for scheduling. Let
$\mathcal{O}$ be the ordered list of the 11 FOGA operators. The within-cycle
mutation attempts are indexed by $(i,o)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\mathcal{O}$:
\[
 Z_{k,i,o}\sim \widetilde K_{k,o}(X_{k,i},\cdot\mid S_k).
\]
This notation denotes a conditional law, not random parent or operator
selection. The inspected implementation uses the deterministic Cartesian
schedule ``parents in stored order, then operators in registry order,'' so
each parent--operator pair is attempted exactly once. A content-derived seed
from $(k,\operatorname{hash}(X_{k,i}),o)$ drives the stochastic operator
parameters and repair. If $Z_{k,i,o}=\partial$, that scheduled attempt produces
no child and is not repeated. Conditioning the kernel on acceptance would
instead describe an implicit-resampling algorithm and is not used here.

Let $\operatorname{FU}$ retain first occurrences in a finite ordered list by
instance hash. Apply it to the subsequence of
$(Z_{k,i,o})_{i,o}$ lying in $\mathcal X$ to obtain the accepted,
child-deduplicated list $O_k$. An accepted duplicate is removed by this
deterministic map; it is not redrawn. Let
$C_k=\operatorname{unique}(P_k\cup O_k)$ be the candidate union, retaining
the first occurrence under the declared order. In statements that treat
hash-based deduplication as mathematical set union, assume that equal hashes
identify the same solver-visible phenotype on the realized finite pool.
For the weaker occupied-niche equality alone, it is sufficient that
equal-hash candidates have the same niche label:
\[
 \operatorname{hash}(x)=\operatorname{hash}(y)
 \ \Longrightarrow\ g(x)=g(y)
 \qquad (x,y\text{ in the pre-deduplication pool}).
\]
Without this compatibility assumption, first-occurrence deduplication could
erase a genuinely different offspring niche. The implementation checks
content-derived hashes operationally; cryptographic collision resistance is
not a literal mathematical injectivity theorem.
At C00 S6-AE reuses the hash-locked corrected union; later cycles construct
this union directly. The
cycle then fits/scores with HGB40, selects the acquisition/next-parent set,
obtains exact labels for that selected set, and only then adapts the BQ solver
from $q_k$ to $q_{k+1}$. Thus within-cycle mutation attempts and solver cycles
must not share one index.

\subsection{Population memory and parent survival}
At BQ cycle $k$, let $P_k$ be the ordered parent population and $A_k$ the
branch-local labeled archive. Let $S_k$
collect scores, lineage information, solver state, and other algorithm
variables that affect the next transition. Thus $M_k=(P_k,A_k,S_k)$ is the
operational memory state. Parent survival is a transition event: for parent
$i$, its survival probability is
\[
 r_{k,i}=\Prb(X_{k,i}\in P_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\rm pre}).
\]
In a generic policy this may differ from a proposal-selection probability.
For S6-AE, however, no such random parent-selection variable is present:
every member of $P_k$ receives all 11 scheduled mutations. Population update
is a deterministic function of the realized candidate union, HGB40 scores,
niche assignments, and hash tie-breaks. Randomness can still enter through
the roots, mutation parameters, calibration sample, model fitting, training
permutations, or nondeterministic numerical execution. The full process may
be time-inhomogeneous Markovian in a sufficiently rich
$(M_k,\theta_k,q_k)$; a niche label or count vector alone need not be.

For a finite candidate set $A$, define its occupied-niche set by
\[
 \mathsf S(A)=\{g(x):x\in A\}.
\]
For a finite unique candidate union $C$ and score $\widehat h$, let
$e_j(C)$ be the maximum-score member of $C\cap\mathcal N_j$, using ascending
instance hash to break ties, and define the per-niche floor
\[
 Q(C)=\{e_j(C):j\in\mathsf S(C)\}.
\]
This set has $|\mathsf S(C)|$ members, not 10,000 members in general. If the
population capacity is $m$, $|\mathsf S(C)|\leq m\leq|C|$, let $F_m(C)$ be the
first $m-|Q(C)|$ candidates of $C\setminus Q(C)$ under global descending
$\widehat h$ and ascending-hash tie order. The complete selected population is
\[
 T_m(C)=Q(C)\cup F_m(C).
\]
The notation denotes the membership set; the implementation also records a
deterministic order.

In verified S6-AE, $m=10{,}000$, $K=1000$, $Q_k=Q(C_k)$, and
$T_k=T_m(C_k)$. The result has 10,000 unique members and is used both as the
BQ acquisition set and next parent population: $P_{k+1}=T_k$.
``Elite-QD1000'' therefore names the occupied-niche floor $Q_k$, not the full
$T_k$. The score can change at every cycle without changing the fixed
partition $g$.

For any $x\in C$, the deterministic membership criterion is
\[
 x\in T_m(C)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x=e_{g(x)}(C)\ \text{or}\
 \bigl[x\notin Q(C)\ \text{and}\
 \operatorname{rank}_{C\setminus Q(C)}(x)\leq m-|Q(C)|\bigr].
\]
Consequently, retaining a niche does not imply retaining any particular
parent hash in that niche.

\subsection{Causal information structure and time-indexed bridge sets}
For each cycle $t$, let $\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$ represent all information
available immediately before resolving the designated mutation batch: the
entering solver state $q_t$, available parents and archive, and any previously
realized random inputs and scores. It must exclude randomness of the batch
still to be drawn under the probability ensemble. Let
$\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm{batch}}$ add the realized accepted/rejected outputs and
candidate union, and let $\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm{score}}$ additionally contain
all scores, niche assignments, and tie-break data necessary for selection.
With the verified deterministic Elite-QD rule, the event that a particular
bridge is retained is measurable with respect to
$\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm{score}}$, and its conditional probability given that
complete information is its indicator (zero or one).
The probabilistic retention premise below is therefore conditioned on the
\emph{pre-batch} information, not the fully resolved selection state.

For a prospective chain starting at cycle $k$, choose measurable bridge sets
$B_0,\ldots,B_L$ causally. The initial set $B_0$ is known at
$\mathcal F_k^{\mathrm{pre}}$. For $\ell=0,\ldots,L-1$, choose the next
set $B_{\ell+1}$ using only $\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\mathrm{pre}}$,
\emph{before} drawing that cycle's batch, and keep its identity fixed until
the next retention step. A measurable random-set formulation is allowed if
its membership event is jointly measurable. Thus $B_\ell$ is already fixed
when it is used as a parent-availability certificate at cycle $k+\ell$.
No bridge set may be chosen retroactively to fit a successful offspring.

Let
\[
 E_\ell=\{P_{k+\ell}\cap B_\ell\neq\varnothing\},\qquad
 G_\ell=\{C_{k+\ell}\cap B_{\ell+1}\neq\varnothing\},
\]
and write
\[
 R_\ell=\{T_{k+\ell}\cap B_{\ell+1}\neq\varnothing\}.
\]
Since $P_{k+\ell+1}=T_{k+\ell}$, $R_\ell=E_{\ell+1}$.
$E_\ell$ is measurable before cycle $k+\ell$'s generation, whereas
$G_\ell$ and $R_\ell$ become measurable after generation and selection.
The bridge-success prefix is $H_0=E_0$ and
$H_{\ell+1}=H_\ell\cap G_\ell\cap R_\ell$.

For terminal correctness, the final $B_L$ must be selected at or before
$\mathcal F_{k+L-1}^{\mathrm{pre}}$, when the entering solver
$q_{k+L-1}$ is known, and satisfy
\[
 B_L\subseteq\mathcal H_{k+L-1}
 \quad\text{almost surely on admissible pre-generation histories}.
\]
This condition allows the final target to depend causally on the current
solver, but forbids selection of $B_L$ using an unseen future checkpoint or
the realized final offspring. If all bridge sets are instead frozen at
cycle $k$, terminal inclusion must hold across the subsequently possible
solver trajectories; this is a stronger, robust-target assumption.

A prospective future-reachability value can be written
\[
 V_{k,L}(x)=\Prb(\text{a declared future true-hard set is hit within $L$
 cycles}\mid x\text{ is retained at cycle }k,\ \mathcal F_k).
\]
This value generally depends on other population members, future selection,
and the adapted solver states. It need not be determined by current BQ
hardness or current HGB prediction.

\subsection{Probability space and frozen executions}
Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\Prb)$ carry exactly the random inputs declared for
the analysis: possibly random initial roots, content-derived operator seeds
and their stochastic parameters/repair, seeded archive calibration sampling,
HGB estimator seeds, BQ training permutations and suffix sampling, and any
explicit selection randomness in a comparator. Conditioning on the frozen
S6-AE roots, source artifacts, software/runtime, and all recorded seeds gives
a single realized trajectory $\omega_0$; many nominal ``probabilities'' then
collapse to zero or one. A probabilistic reachability theorem must therefore
state which inputs vary across $\Omega$. Repeatedly serializing a deterministic
batch does not create random parent/operator selection, and unspecified
hardware nondeterminism is not silently treated as a scientific sampling law.

\subsection{Hardness and moving target}
Let $\theta_k$ describe the task/environment and $q_k$ the state of the
co-adapting BQ solver before cycle-$k$ adaptation. Define true BQ hardness
$h_k^{\rm BQ}(x)=h(x;\theta_k,q_k)$,
where a larger value means harder under a prespecified solver-performance
metric. For threshold $\kappa$, the true hard region at cycle $k$ is
\[
 \HARD_k=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:h_k^{\rm BQ}(x)\geq\kappa\}.
\]
This definition allows the target to move because either the task or solver
state changes. During the S6-AE mutation/acquisition part of cycle $k$, $q_k$
is fixed; adaptation produces $q_{k+1}$ only after $T_k$ is frozen and labeled.
For the proofs, the target attached to a declared opportunity must be
predictable from its pre-opportunity history.

HGB40 supplies a cycle-specific prediction $\widehat h_k(x)$ (in the
implementation, predicted $\log(1+\mathrm{LBQ})$), not the definition of
$h_k^{\rm BQ}$ or $\HARD_k$. Define the latent generation-level event
\[
 D_k^{\rm gen}=\{\exists x\in C_k: h_k^{\rm BQ}(x)\geq\kappa\},
\]
and the acquisition-level event
\[
 D_k^{\rm acq}=\{\exists x\in T_k: h_k^{\rm BQ}(x)\geq\kappa\}.
\]
The former concerns what the generation-level candidate union contains,
including retained parents and accepted children, even when exact hardness
was not measured; the latter concerns what survived HGB/QD acquisition and can be
verified. They are distinct from the event
$\{\widehat h_k(x)\geq\widehat\kappa\}$ and from post-adaptation hardness
$h_{k+1}^{\rm BQ}(x)$. Without a calibration/ranking-and-retention condition,
none of these quantities can be substituted for another.

\subsection{Presence, novelty, and reactivation}
The events $D_k^{\rm gen}$ and $D_k^{\rm acq}$ test \emph{presence}; the
parent-inclusive union can satisfy them using an old instance without
creating a new one. Let $\mathcal V_{k-1}^{\rm gen}
=\bigcup_{t<k}C_t$ and $\mathcal V_{k-1}^{\rm acq}
=\bigcup_{t<k}T_t$ denote previously generated and acquired phenotype sets,
interpreted under the declared hash/phenotype compatibility rule.
For a mathematically new, valid offspring of current true hardness at least
$\kappa$, exclude both previously generated phenotypes and the \emph{current}
parent population (including the initial roots of cycle 0):
\[
 D_k^{\rm new,gen}
 =\left\{\exists x\in
 O_k\setminus(\mathcal V_{k-1}^{\rm gen}\cup P_k):
 h_k^{\rm BQ}(x)\geq\kappa\right\}.
\]
This exclusion is essential when a valid mutation returns the identical
parent phenotype or when its child hash duplicates another current parent.
It does not alter the parent-inclusive presence event $D_k^{\rm gen}$.
A new acquisition under the cumulative acquisition ledger is
\[
 D_k^{\rm new,acq}
 =\{\exists x\in T_k\setminus\mathcal V_{k-1}^{\rm acq}:
               h_k^{\rm BQ}(x)\geq\kappa\}.
\]
A newly generated \emph{and} acquired hard instance satisfies
\[
 D_k^{\rm new,gen\cap acq}=
 \left\{\exists x\in
 (O_k\cap T_k)\setminus
 (\mathcal V_{k-1}^{\rm gen}\cup P_k):
 h_k^{\rm BQ}(x)\geq\kappa\right\}.
\]
This is a stricter event than $D_k^{\rm new,acq}$: an old parent may be
selected for the first time, but is not thereby a newly generated phenotype.
An old instance may become hard under $q_k$ after being non-hard under an
earlier checkpoint; this is \emph{hardness reactivation}, not instance
novelty. A previously unvisited hard niche is a different event again.
The baseline bridge-chain theorem below concerns \emph{presence}, not these
stronger novelty events; it may not be cited as a novelty-discovery bound
without additional exclusion conditions.

\subsection{First hit and computational normalization}
For the abstract survival lemma, index declared comparison opportunities by
$j$; $j$ is not automatically a BQ cycle. An opportunity may be one serialized
mutation attempt, one full generation event $D_k^{\rm gen}$, or one
acquisition event $D_k^{\rm acq}$, but the choice must be fixed before the
comparison. Let
\[
 \tau=\inf\{j\geq 1:Y_j=1\},\qquad
 C_\tau=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}c_j,
\]
where $Y_j$ is the declared true-hardness discovery event and $c_j>0$ is the
normalized compute cost through resolving opportunity $j$.
The budget-$B$ discovery event is $\{C_\tau\leq B\}$. The cost must include
the work that differs between methods (structural generation, HGB40 inference,
archive operations, and BQ evaluation/verification, as applicable). Counting
generations alone is not computational normalization. The elementary bound
below uses deterministic opportunity costs $c_i$; random or method-dependent
costs require a cost-aware hazard analysis and are not silently replaced by
generation counts.

For a policy $\pi$, define its conditional hit hazard
\[
 q_j^\pi=\Prb(Y_j=1\mid\mathcal{F}_{j-1}^\pi),
\]
where $\mathcal{F}_{j-1}^\pi$ is the complete pre-opportunity history. On a
no-hit history, a lower bound on $q_j^\pi$ is a reachability certificate. A
Markov state representation is one way to calculate this hazard, but the
history-based definition does not require Markovianity.

\subsection{Niche aggregation and the Markov condition}
Suppose a Markov kernel $Q$ on a full state space is aggregated by a finite
measurable partition $\{B_1,\ldots,B_L\}$. The strong-lumpability equalities
\[
 Q(s,B_j)=Q(s',B_j)\quad
 \text{for every }s,s'\in B_i\text{ and every }i,j
\]
are sufficient for the aggregate block process to be Markov for every initial
law. They are not necessary for a particular initial law. A weaker,
distribution-dependent Markov aggregation may hold when the conditional
within-block law induced by that initial distribution and history makes the
averaged transition to each $B_j$ history-independent (possibly with a
time-dependent aggregate kernel). Such a claim must name that law and prove
the conditional equality; it is not obtained merely by averaging once.

The counterexample in Section~\ref{sec:counterexamples} shows failure of the
strong condition and therefore invalidates an implementation-independent
Markov claim based on niche alone. It does not rule out a special weak
aggregation for a specified initial distribution. In S6-AE, niche summaries
hide parent identity, within-niche HGB order, candidate age, solver/archive
state, and the deterministic hash tie-break, so strong lumpability is not
established and a distribution-dependent alternative remains OPEN.
