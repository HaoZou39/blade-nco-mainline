\begin{abstract}
This note formalizes structural reachability, inherited population memory, moving hard-instance regions, and first discovery measured in computational cost. It records three candidate claims, attempts a proof of a finite-window memory comparison, and tests broad memory/diversity claims against exact finite-state examples. The strongest surviving result is conditional: retained structural memory helps only when it supplies a verified per-cost hit-hazard advantage over the comparator. No unconditional BLADE-NCO advantage is established. This document is a cumulative Stages 1--3B mathematical research note, not a completed theory paper.

Stage 1.5 corrects the state space, time indices, probability space,
aggregation language, and implementation mapping without adding a theorem or
changing that conclusion. The finite examples remain counterexamples to
universal claims; they are not assumptions that the TSP instance space is
finite.

Stage 2 proves a deterministic occupied-niche persistence invariant for the
verified parent--child union and Elite-QD selector, while showing by exact
counterexample that niche persistence need not preserve useful structural
representatives. It also gives a finite-budget multistep bridge-chain bound
whose factors are operator-specific accepted-transition and selection-retention
certificates. The theorem is mathematically valid under its stated premises;
the decisive BLADE-specific kernel and bridge-retention premises remain open.

Stage 2.1 independently corrects the information set for deterministic versus
probabilistic bridge retention, imposes a non-anticipating choice of
cycle-indexed bridge targets, makes hash-deduplication compatibility explicit,
and separates hard-instance presence from new-instance discovery. These are
corrections to the conditional framework, not additional superiority theorems.

Stage 3A proves a standard per-niche approximate-argmax guarantee and its
one-step solver-drift extension. It also derives a finite-horizon historical
tracking-regret recurrence under a substantially stronger drift bound over
the complete latent candidate history. The recurrence can accumulate linearly,
and its complete-pool HGB-error and historical-drift premises are not verified
for S6-AE. Exact counterexamples continue to separate current-hardness tracking
from future reachability and downstream BQ training value.

Stage 3B removes those unsupported premises and audits what is observable.
It proves an exact exchange identity between Elite-QD acquisition and pure
global HGB Top10k, making the surrogate-score opportunity cost computable.
An exact finite-pool indistinguishability construction shows that selectively
verified labels do not identify full-pool niche maxima, elite true-hardness
regret, hard-tail recall, or the true Elite-QD-versus-global comparison.  The
missing paired comparison can be completed with at most 1,000 same-checkpoint
labels, but those labels are not present in the frozen artifacts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and status}
The scope is restricted to structural reachability, evolutionary memory, and expected first-hitting time under a non-stationary hardness landscape. The supplied BLADE-NCO context is FOGA-11 structural mutation, inherited parent populations, HGB40 hardness estimation, Elite-QD1000 (P=T), and BQ solver co-adaptation. Those names are treated as labels, not as complete algorithm specifications.

Statuses are reported on three separate axes. \mstatus{PROVED}, for example,
concerns the validity of a mathematical statement under its premises;
\istatus{VERIFIED, PARTIAL, OPEN, or NOT APPLICABLE} concerns whether those
premises and variables match the inspected S6-AE implementation; and
\nstatus{OPEN or NOT CLAIMED} concerns literature priority. A proof of a
conditional probability bound does not establish implementation applicability
or novelty. Compilation verifies only that the source typesets; it does not
verify mathematical correctness.

The Stage 1.5--2 implementation boundary is the verified HGB40 + Elite-QD1000
$(P=T)$ S6-AE path. Its frozen artifacts passed 40/40 strict checks. Historical
GP4 output is not used as the definition of the current method. Details that
could not be established from the inspected code and artifacts are marked
OPEN rather than inferred.

\section{Prior-theory boundary}
Related theory includes rigorous results in which populations track moving
optima and in which diversity mechanisms have different effects, but those
works analyze explicit dynamic-function classes and specified algorithm
dynamics \cite{Dang2017,OlivetoZarges2015}. MAP-Elites and QD explicitly store
high-quality representatives in a fixed feature partition
\cite{MouretClune2015,Pugh2016}; recent runtime analyses prove coverage and
optimization results for particular pseudo-Boolean and combinatorial
structures \cite{BossekSudholt2024,Qian2024}. These works make the elementary
archive-occupancy invariant non-novel, and they show that stepping-stone
benefits require an aligned problem/operator model. They do not establish
compute-normalized discovery of solver-relative hard TSP instances under
surrogate selection and solver co-adaptation. Whether a fully instantiated
BLADE-specific bridge theorem would be novel remains \nstatus{OPEN}; the
present limited audit is not a comprehensive priority search.

\section{Research assessment}
\paragraph{Strongest candidate.}
The strongest Stage 2 candidate is Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge-chain}. It
turns a multicycle structural path into an auditable finite-budget bound using
operator-specific accepted-transition masses and explicit selection-retention
certificates. Unlike Proposition~\ref{prop:window}, it does not begin by
assuming an unexplained overall hazard advantage. Proposition
~\ref{prop:niche-persistence} supplies a verified structural invariant, but
the progressive-loss counterexample shows why that invariant cannot supply
the bridge-retention premise by itself.

\paragraph{Limitations and novelty.}
The product/conditioning argument and archive occupancy fact are standard and
are not claimed as new theory. Scientific content would have to come from
deriving non-vacuous FOGA-11 kernel bounds, proving that current-HGB elites
retain future-relevant bridges despite BQ drift, and obtaining a strict
compute-normalized separation from specified comparators. The inspected
implementation verifies the deterministic operator schedule, rejection
without resampling, population update, capacity relation, and $P=T$, but not
those probabilistic or score--reachability premises. The result therefore does
not assert a BLADE advantage.

Stage 2 verifies the S6-AE batch schedule and population update rule, but not
the required probabilistic hazard bounds, a scientific hard-region threshold,
or a matched HAC transition/cost law. Therefore the mathematical comparison
remains conditional, its BLADE applicability remains partial, and its novelty
remains open.

\paragraph{Testability.}
The structural persistence proposition is checkable directly from the
selector definition and existing artifacts. The bridge theorem would require
certified support or lower bounds for accepted operator transitions between
declared bridge sets and proof that the selector retains those sets. The
inspected artifacts record proposals, rejections, scores, and memberships, but
a single frozen trajectory cannot establish history-uniform kernel bounds.
\par\istatus{OPEN}\par
No new evaluations, training, or evolutionary runs were performed for this
note.

\paragraph{Unresolved issues.}
The decisive open issue is whether current-hardness selection preserves
representatives with high future-reachability value often enough to complete a
nontrivial bridge chain. Exact operator laws under a declared ensemble,
score--bridge alignment, target sets, and matched fixed-generator/reset/HAC
cost models remain necessary before a BLADE-specific comparison theorem can
be claimed.

\paragraph{Stage 3A tracking assessment.}
The one-step $2\epsilon$ quality guarantee and
$2\epsilon+2\delta$ solver-drift guarantee are mathematically valid but
standard. Parent--child union and $P=T$ keep the immediately previous niche
elite available, which supports a historical regret recurrence. That
recurrence requires current-error bounds on the complete candidate niche and
drift bounds on every candidate in the latent historical niche ledger; S6-AE
does not label those complete sets. Bounded per-cycle terms permit linear
regret, not a steady-state guarantee. The Stage 3A decision is therefore
\textbf{C -- TRACKING\_ASSUMPTIONS\_NOT\_JUSTIFIED}.

\paragraph{Stage 3B identifiability assessment.}
The verified selector satisfies
$T_k\setminus G_k=Q_k\setminus G_k$ and exchanges at most 1,000 members with
pure global HGB Top10k.  Its predicted-score cost is nonnegative and exactly
observable.  True current hardness, however, is selective: all of $T_k$ is
labeled, but most displaced members of $G_k\setminus T_k$ and most of the
candidate union are not.  Two truth assignments can therefore agree on every
saved observable while reversing the full-pool conclusions.  This is an
information limit, not evidence of poor realized selection, and the Stage 3B
decision is \textbf{B -- IDENTIFIABILITY\_LIMIT\_CONFIRMED}.

\section{Stopping point}
Stage 1 formalization, Stage 1.5 alignment, Stage 2 persistence/reachability,
Stage 2.1 independent mathematical corrections, Stage 3A solver-relative
hardness tracking, and Stage 3B observability analysis are recorded. The
revised bridge theorem is a conditional, pre-generation-information
certificate, not an implemented or empirically verified BLADE superiority
result. The Stage 3A regret recurrence is likewise conditional and does not
establish bounded long-run tracking.  Stage 3B specifies, but does not run, a
minimal same-checkpoint verification protocol. No experiment, algorithm
change, Oracle/BQ evaluation, retraining, adaptation, evolution, or Stage 3C
development is included.
