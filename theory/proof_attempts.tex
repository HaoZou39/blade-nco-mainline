\section{Proof attempt for the strongest candidate}

\subsection{History-based survival lemma}
\mstatus{PROVED} \istatus{PARTIAL} \nstatus{NOT CLAIMED} The lemma below is
a standard finite-horizon hitting bound,
included as the proof engine rather than as a novelty claim.

Let $Y_j$ be the indicator of the declared discovery event at comparison
opportunity $j$ and let $\tau=\inf\{j\geq1:Y_j=1\}$. The event
$\{\tau\geq j\}$ is determined by the history before opportunity $j$.
Assume there are deterministic numbers $p_j\in[0,1]$ such that, on every
pre-opportunity history with no previous hit,
\[
 \Prb(Y_j=1\mid\mathcal{F}_{j-1})\geq p_j.
\]
This is a history-uniform conditional hazard condition. It allows the target
attached to the event to vary with $j$. The index $j$ is deliberately not the
BQ cycle index $k$: it may represent a serialized mutation attempt, a full
generation event $D_k^{\rm gen}$, or an acquisition event
$D_k^{\rm acq}$, provided the choice and filtration are fixed.

\begin{lemma}[Non-stationary survival and cost bound]
For every $n\geq1$,
\[
 \Prb(\tau>n)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p_i).
\]
If opportunity costs $c_i$ are deterministic, then
\[
 \Ex[C_\tau]
 \leq \sum_{i\geq1}c_i\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}(1-p_j),
\]
with the inequality valid also when the right side is infinite. For a budget $B$, assume
$\sum_{i=1}^\infty c_i=\infty$ and let
$n(B)=\max\{n\geq0: \sum_{i=1}^{n}c_i\leq B\}$, with empty sum zero.
\[
 \Prb(C_\tau\leq B)\geq
 1-\prod_{i=1}^{n(B)}(1-p_i).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $S_i=\{\tau\geq i\}$ for survival to the start of opportunity $i$.
Since $S_i\in\mathcal{F}_{i-1}$,
\begin{align*}
 \Prb(S_{i+1})
 &=\Ex\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{S_i}
       \Prb(Y_i=0\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\right] \\
 &\leq (1-p_i)\Prb(S_i).
\end{align*}
Starting from $\Prb(S_1)=1$ and iterating gives
$\Prb(S_{n+1})\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p_i)$, which is the first claim.
For the cost, use the nonnegative-sum identity
\[
 C_\tau=\sum_{i\geq1}c_i\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\geq i\}},
 \qquad
 \Ex[C_\tau]=\sum_{i\geq1}c_i\Prb(S_i),
\]
and apply the survival bound termwise. Finally, completion of any of the
first $n(B)$ opportunities costs at most $B$, so budget success is at least the
probability of a hit among those opportunities. The first claim supplies the
stated lower bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Deriving the window comparison}
For Candidate~1, apply the survival lemma on the $W$-opportunity stable-target
window. The memory policy satisfies $p_j=p_M$ and the comparator satisfies
an upper hazard bound $q_j^G\leq p_G$ on every no-hit history. The same
induction, with the inequality reversed for $G$, yields
\[
 \Prb(\tau_M>j)\leq(1-p_M)^j,
 \qquad
 \Prb(\tau_G>j)\geq(1-p_G)^j.
\]
Therefore, for $n\leq W$,
\[
 \Prb(\tau_M\leq n)\geq1-(1-p_M)^n
 >1-(1-p_G)^n\geq\Prb(\tau_G\leq n).
\]
For $2\leq n\leq W$, using
$\Ex[\min(\tau,n)]=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Prb(\tau>j)$ and multiplying by the
common opportunity cost $c$ gives the strict truncated compute-cost comparison in
Proposition~\ref{prop:window}. At $n=1$ both policies pay the first opportunity
cost in the truncated-cost convention, so only the success-probability
comparison is strict there.

\subsection{Stage 2 proof of deterministic niche persistence}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:niche-persistence}]
For every $j\in\mathsf S(C_k)$, the intersection
$C_k\cap\mathcal N_j$ is nonempty, so $e_j(C_k)$ exists. By construction,
$e_j(C_k)\in Q(C_k)\subseteq T_k=P_{k+1}$. Hence
$\mathsf S(C_k)\subseteq\mathsf S(P_{k+1})$. Conversely $T_k\subseteq C_k$,
so $\mathsf S(P_{k+1})\subseteq\mathsf S(C_k)$ and equality holds. Since
$P_k\subseteq C_k$, monotonicity follows. If $C_k$ is the unique union of
$P_k$ and $O_k$, applying $g$ gives
$\mathsf S(C_k)=\mathsf S(P_k)\cup\mathsf S(O_k)$. A rejected outcome is
$\partial\notin\mathcal X$ and is absent from $O_k$, so it changes none of
these inclusions. No claim about which member realizes a niche is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:membership}]
The two components $Q(C)$ and $F_m(C)$ are disjoint. The first contains exactly
the declared score-and-hash winner in each occupied niche. The second is, by
definition, exactly the prefix of length $m-|Q(C)|$ of the global order on the
remaining candidates. Their union therefore yields the stated necessary and
sufficient condition. A previous parent that loses both tests is absent from
$T_m(C)$ even though another member of its niche is present.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stage 2.1: corrected proof of the causal bridge-chain bound}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge-chain}]
By non-anticipation, $H_\ell$ and the two bridge sets used in cycle
$k+\ell$ are known at $\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre}$; in particular the
terminal set is selected before its final mutation batch. Conditional on
any positive-probability pre-generation history in $H_\ell$, the ordered
conditional-probability identity gives
\begin{align*}
 &\Prb(G_\ell\cap R_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre})\\
 &\quad=
 \Prb(G_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre})\,
 \Prb(R_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre},G_\ell)
 \geq a_\ell\rho_\ell.
\end{align*}
The event-conditioned factor is only required on histories where the first
factor is positive. In the degenerate case $a_\ell=0$, the desired lower
bound is zero without any retention condition. No independence or
random parent/operator selection is used.

Since $H_\ell\in\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre}$, the tower property yields
\[
 \Prb(H_{\ell+1})
 =\Ex\!\left[\mathbf 1_{H_\ell}
       \Prb(G_\ell\cap R_\ell\mid
                     \mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre})\right]
 \geq a_\ell\rho_\ell\Prb(H_\ell).
\]
Starting with $\Prb(H_0)=1$ and iterating through $\ell=L-2$
gives
\[
 \Prb(H_{L-1})\geq
       \prod_{\ell=0}^{L-2}a_\ell\rho_\ell.
\]
In the last cycle, pre-generation target correctness is
$B_L\subseteq\HARD_{k+L-1}$ almost surely on admissible histories.
Thus $H_{L-1}\cap G_{L-1}$ implies a current-solver true-hard instance
is present in the final candidate union, and has probability at least
$\Prb(H_{L-1})a_{L-1}$. Requiring $R_{L-1}$ as well yields the
acquisition-level factor $\rho_{L-1}$.

For the operator-specific sufficient condition, choose a bridge parent
from $P_{k+\ell}\cap B_\ell$ by a fixed measurable rule (for example the
least hash, assuming the hash convention distinguishes needed
phenotypes). Because all eleven operators are scheduled, the designated
operator is attempted on that parent. Its full, rejection-aware
conditional kernel assigns mass at least $a_\ell$ to $B_{\ell+1}$.
Provided that hash deduplication identifies equal solver-visible
phenotypes on the realized pool (or at least preserves membership in
$B_{\ell+1}$), an accepted bridge result remains represented in the
candidate union even if it duplicates an earlier occurrence.
This establishes the generation premise.

Once the complete scored candidate union is conditioned upon, $R_\ell$
is deterministic and $\Prb(R_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm score})
=\mathbf 1_{R_\ell}$. Therefore a pathwise elite/global-fill membership
certificate guarantees $\rho_\ell=1$. A fractional $\rho_\ell$ can only
be a conditional average over randomness \emph{not} already revealed by
$\mathcal F_{k+\ell}^{\rm pre}$, and is not implied by the deterministic
selector itself. The cost statement follows when the total charged cost
of the completed certified chain is bounded by $B$ as assumed.
\end{proof}

For repeated certified blocks, condition on each no-hit block boundary.
An explicit regeneration hypothesis must restore both the initial bridge
availability and the same conditional transition/retention bounds.
The history-based survival lemma then gives survival at most $(1-p)^r$
after $r$ blocks and expected cost at most $C/p$ if the cost of each
block is bounded above by $C$. No regeneration claim is made for S6-AE.

\subsection{Dependency audit}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Conditional independence:} not used. The proof requires
history-conditional lower/upper hazard bounds; dependence across offspring or
generations is allowed if those bounds remain true after every no-hit history.
\item \textbf{Markovianity:} not used in the hitting proof. It would only be
needed if one chooses a finite-state transition matrix to calculate the
hazards. A niche aggregation is valid for that purpose only under lumpability.
\item \textbf{Mutation bounds:} the quantity $\alpha$ must be a lower bound
on reaching the true current hard region conditional on the useful parent
being selected. A graph path's existence alone is insufficient; its
probability and compute cost matter.
The Stage 2 bridge bound instead exposes operator-specific accepted-output
mass in $\widetilde K_{k,o}$; rejection probability remains in the denominator
and no accepted-only renormalization is permitted.
\item \textbf{Parent retention and scheduling:} a lower bound $r$ must concern
availability of a genuinely useful parent at the declared opportunity.
Historical presence before a target shift does not establish continued
usefulness afterward. The S6-AE parent/operator schedule is deterministic:
conditional on $P_k$, each parent receives all 11 operators. Hence no random
parent-selection factor $\gamma$ may be inserted; a batch reachability bound
$\alpha_{\rm batch}$ must be derived under the declared RNG ensemble.
\item \textbf{Causal target choice and retention information:} the bridge
sets must be fixed before their respective generation batches. The last
target is the true hard region of the \emph{entering} solver checkpoint.
Selection is deterministic after scoring; a nontrivial fractional
$\rho_\ell$ must be conditioned on a coarser, pre-generation sigma-algebra,
or replaced with a pathwise rank certificate.
\item \textbf{Hash-quotient semantics:} niche occupancy only needs
equal-hash candidates to share a niche, but bridge-chain propagation
additionally needs equal hashes to identify equivalent phenotypes or
at least to preserve membership in every chosen bridge set.
\item \textbf{Surrogate accuracy:} no accuracy is needed for the abstract
survival lemma. To pass from $D_k^{\rm gen}$ to
$D_k^{\rm acq}$, a retention condition connecting $\widehat h_k$ and the QD
rule to true $h_k^{\rm BQ}$ and future reachability is essential. Perfect
current-hardness prediction alone is insufficient when tied candidates have
different mutation futures. Without such a bridge, the implementation-level
step is OPEN.
\item \textbf{Capacity and selection:} niche persistence uses
$|\mathsf S(C_k)|\leq m$ and inclusion of the complete floor $Q(C_k)$. It does
not imply bridge persistence. The latter requires the exact elite/global-fill
membership condition or another proved retention certificate.
\item \textbf{Changing hardness:} the bridge theorem allows cycle-indexed
kernels and sets, but $B_L\subseteq\HARD_{k+L-1}$ must refer to true hardness
under the entering checkpoint for the final acquisition cycle. Current HGB
quality cannot silently replace that condition.
\item \textbf{Cost normalization:} the comparison assumes equal deterministic
cost $c$ per opportunity. If BLADE archive updates, HGB40 inference, or BQ
validation change cost, opportunity-count dominance does not imply compute-cost
dominance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Failure boundary}
\mstatus{OPEN for a BLADE-specific dominance claim}
\istatus{PARTIAL} \nstatus{OPEN} The proof does not establish that BLADE's conditional hazard is
above the fixed-generator or HAC hazard. It fails to produce a BLADE result
at the exact step where one would need verified bounds on $r$ and
$\alpha_{\rm batch}$ (or a direct generation/acquisition hazard bound) and a
comparator upper bound $p_G$, all in the same compute units. The
counterexamples show these inequalities can reverse. No unproved transition
from a structural-memory narrative to hazard dominance is made.
